# 1ofN Outcome Follow-up

This is an optional evaluation worksheet for learning from a real case after a 1ofN decision has produced observable results.

It is **not**:

- a sixth 1ofN stage;
- a requirement for completing `DECIDE`;
- a scoring or probability system;
- permission to rewrite the original decision after seeing the outcome.

Use it only when there is a meaningful later result to observe.

## Why keep the follow-up separate?

Decision quality at decision time and the realized outcome are related, but they are not identical. A defensible decision can encounter bad luck or an unexpected environment change. A weak decision can also get lucky.

The follow-up therefore preserves two different questions:

1. Was the original decision defensible from the evidence available at the time?
2. What did reality later reveal that should improve future runs?

## 1. Freeze the original record

Before evaluating the outcome, copy or reference what the original run actually recorded:

- decision and desired outcome;
- selected path;
- evidence basis;
- important assumptions and unknowns;
- residual uncertainty;
- reversal or reopen conditions;
- expected result, effort, cost, or timing **only if the original run explicitly stated them**.

Do not invent missing forecasts and do not backfill a prediction after the outcome is known.

## 2. Record observed reality

Capture only what can now be supported:

- observation date or window;
- observed result;
- actual effort, cost, or timing when relevant and known;
- material surprises;
- assumptions that held;
- assumptions that failed;
- whether any stated reversal or reopen condition was triggered;
- evidence or references supporting the observation.

If causality, luck, noise, or attribution cannot be distinguished, leave that uncertainty visible.

## 3. Separate outcome from decision-process quality

Evaluate the original run without outcome hindsight:

- Was the frame correct enough for the decision?
- Were material candidates visible?
- Was contrary evidence tested?
- Did every candidate receive a traceable verdict?
- Were uncertainty and reversal conditions visible?
- Would the same evidence available at the time still justify the decision?

Then evaluate the later outcome separately.

Do not mark a sound decision as unsound merely because the result was unfavorable. Do not mark a weak decision as sound merely because the result was favorable.

## 4. Create a learning candidate, not an instant rule

State what the case suggests should be tested in future runs.

A single case may justify a new hypothesis, example, failure case, or evaluation case. It does not by itself change the constitutional method.

Repeated patterns should go through the repository's normal growth path before becoming a canonical capability or rule.
